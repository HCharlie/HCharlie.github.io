# Editorial Principles

This site should make technical knowledge easy to enter, useful to apply, and easy to finish. Public articles provide the clearest useful path through a topic; deeper material remains available without becoming required reading.

## Write for a calm reading experience

- Give each article one clear purpose.
- Start with the simplest useful model.
- Use plain language, short paragraphs, and descriptive headings.
- Introduce structure only when it helps readers connect, apply, or remember ideas.
- Prefer a natural explanatory flow over an exhaustive catalogue.
- Keep background material proportional to the question being answered.

A reader should be able to stop at the end of the article with a complete and useful understanding. The article must not depend on a deep-dive link to explain its central idea.

## Make depth optional

Keep the main article focused on the mental model, practical reasoning, and conclusions. Move material such as the following into an optional deep dive when it would interrupt that path:

- extensive implementation details
- source code and runnable examples
- benchmark methodology and raw results
- exhaustive combinations or comparison matrices
- long derivations and supporting evidence
- detailed references and research notes

Choose the deep-dive format that fits the material:

- **GitHub repository:** executable code, experiments, examples, or work that benefits from version history
- **Documentation page or appendix:** stable explanatory material that belongs with the site
- **Notebook:** analysis whose calculations and outputs should remain inspectable
- **External primary source:** authoritative specifications, papers, or product documentation

## Link with clear expectations

Place deep-dive links near the idea they support or in a short section at the end. Name the destination and explain its value so readers can decide whether to continue.

Prefer labels such as:

- `Deep dive: benchmark methodology and raw results (GitHub)`
- `Implementation example: runnable delivery workflow (GitHub)`
- `Further reading: OCI image specification`

The link is an invitation, not an assignment. Avoid implying that readers must follow every reference to understand the article.

## Review before publishing

Confirm that:

- the title and opening establish one clear purpose;
- the simplest useful model appears before details;
- every section contributes to the article's purpose;
- the main conclusion stands without optional links;
- deep dives have an appropriate home and descriptive label;
- the page remains readable on desktop and mobile;
- the article feels finishable rather than exhaustive.
